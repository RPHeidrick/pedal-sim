Pedal library

My own pedal designs and classic reference circuits, simulated component by component. Add as many as your computer can run. Want to try your own? Load an LTspice schematic on the engine bench.

How it works

  1. Schematic in. Each pedal starts as an LTspice schematic. The importer reads the .asc file, traces the wires into a netlist, and turns swept resistors into knobs.
  2. Circuit solved. A modified nodal analysis engine models every resistor, capacitor, diode, transistor and op amp, and solves the nonlinear circuit with Newton iteration at up to eight times the audio rate, so distortion stays free of digital aliasing.
  3. Sound out. The solver runs on the browser's real-time audio thread, so your guitar goes through the circuit as you play. Knobs change the actual pot resistances, and a speaker cabinet model stands in for the amp.

Want the numbers? The engine bench shows operating points, transfer curves and harmonic distortion for any circuit, including your own LTspice files.

Sound check

Pedal Sim starts quietly on purpose. Turn it up a little at a time until the guitar sounds comfortable, never loud. Guitar effects can jump in volume, and headphones put that right in your ears.

What are you listening on?

    Where to turn it up, in this order

    1. Your computer or phone volume. The speaker icon in the taskbar or menu bar, or the volume keys. Start around a quarter.
    2. The Volume slider here (it is also in the Output panel):
    3. The knob on your speakers, headphone amp or audio interface, if you have one. Keep it low until the sound is playing.
